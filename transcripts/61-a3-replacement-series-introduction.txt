# Video 61 — A3 replacement series: scope and test matrix

Cleaned technical transcript. Greetings, repetition, purchasing chatter, and presentation filler removed.

## 0:10–1:47 — Why start with iPhone

The author chooses iPhone as the simpler platform because it has one A3 controller, while a MacBook can have three or four. The same replacement questions therefore exist on MacBook, but mapping and position make them more complex.

Questions to test:
- Can an A3 be swapped between devices with the same part number?
- Must the donor come from the same model?
- Does controller position matter?
- Must the external ROM be transferred with the A3?
- Can controllers be swapped across related models?

The initial test platform is iPhone 15 / 15 Plus.

## 4:47–6:45 — Simple swaps may appear to work but still be wrong

The author warns that A3 swaps can appear fully functional while still having deeper restore/update or serialization problems. The goal of the series is to test those hidden failure modes rather than judge only by charging, touch, or normal USB operation.

## 7:34–9:50 — Two observed A3 variants

Two controller markings are discussed:
- **SN2027 / SN2012027 family**
- **P2027 / P20127 family** as transcribed

The author found both in donor iPhone boards. In his small sample, P variants were common and not restricted to a single geographic region.

A major test question is whether an **SN can be replaced by a P, or vice versa**.

## 10:01–12:30 — ROM and touch

The series will also test the common repair claim that replacing A3 without moving the external ROM causes:
- loss of touch,
- loss of charging,
- loss of USB communication.

The external ROM is therefore treated as a separate variable from the A3 silicon itself.

For iPhone 16 Pro, the author notes that programmers exist which can access/reprogram the A3 ROM through Apple-provided test points without separating the sandwich board.

## 13:08–13:47 — Relevance to MacBook

The author explicitly says the iPhone findings should also matter to MacBook repair. iPhone is the simpler case with one A3; MacBook is expected to be more complicated because it contains multiple A3 controllers.
