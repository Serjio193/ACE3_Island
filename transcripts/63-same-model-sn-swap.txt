# Video 63 — Same-model, same-revision A3 swap

Cleaned technical transcript. Greetings, reballing/soldering dead time, music, repetition, and UI narration removed.

## Test setup

An **SN-family A3** is moved from one iPhone 15 Plus to another iPhone 15 Plus that originally also used an SN-family A3.

The donor A3 is installed **without moving its external ROM**.

Expected initial state: the recipient ROM does not match the donor A3, so the A3 should fail to boot normally and expose PortDFU.

## 10:58–13:16 — PortDFU identity follows the donor A3

After installation:
- the phone boots at the AP level,
- original-screen touch is absent,
- the host sees the device in PortDFU/DFU,
- 3uTools identifies it as iPhone 15 Plus.

The PortDFU ECID observed on the recipient matches the ECID previously seen when that A3 was still installed in the donor phone.

This is the first strong observation in the series that **PortDFU identity travels with the A3 controller**, not with the recipient logic board.

The author phrases this as the ECID being “inside” or personalized into the A3; the direct observation is that the PortDFU identifier follows the transplanted A3.

## 13:54–18:24 — Restore succeeds without moving donor ROM

Apple restore succeeds.

After the restore:
- touch works,
- USB communication works,
- charging/normal operation appear normal,
- the recipient’s existing external ROM has been programmed with firmware usable by the donor A3.

No donor ROM transfer was required.

This confirms the previous experiment: when the A3/device combination is accepted, restore can regenerate the external A3 firmware.

## 16:18–19:55 — Two identity views

After normal boot, 3uTools shows the recipient phone’s normal ECID.

In recovery mode, the recipient phone ECID is also shown.

When the phone is put back into PortDFU, the ECID changes back to the identifier associated with the donor A3.

Observed split:

```
normal boot / recovery
    → recipient phone/AP identity

PortDFU
    → donor A3 identity
```

## 20:03–21:59 — Serialization conclusion

From a user-function perspective the device appears normal:
- touch works,
- USB works,
- charging works,
- restore works.

But the A3 retains a traceable identity from the donor board.

The author concludes that the A3 is **personalized/serialized to the device it originally came from**, because Apple can potentially compare:
- PortDFU identity from the A3,
- normal device identity from the AP.

For this same-model, same-revision swap, Apple restore still succeeds at the time of the experiment, but the author warns that future software could choose to enforce the mismatch.
