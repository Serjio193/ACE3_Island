# Video 66 — AliExpress “decrypted” / unidentified A3

Cleaned technical transcript. Greetings, button-count repetition, restore waiting time, UI narration, and non-technical commentary removed.

## 0:07–2:03 — Test setup

The author tests an AliExpress A3 sold as a “decrypted” replacement.

Marking shown:
- **SN2027 A12** as transcribed.

The original iPhone 15 Plus configuration is restored as close to stock as possible before the test, including its original external ROM contents.

The replacement chip is installed **without transferring a donor ROM**.

## 6:01–7:08 — Works immediately with existing ROM

Unexpectedly, the replacement A3 boots with the ROM already on the phone.

Observed immediately:
- charging response,
- touch works,
- USB communication works,
- normal phone serial/device information is visible.

This differs from ordinary personalized donor A3 chips, which normally expose PortDFU when the A3 and ROM do not match.

## 7:08–11:00 — Manual DFU entry fails

Repeated attempts to enter DFU using the normal button sequence fail.

The device can enter recovery, but the A3 does not appear to enter PortDFU through the ordinary manual sequence.

## 11:00–13:07 — Apple restore succeeds

A standard Apple restore is performed and succeeds.

After restore:
- touch works,
- USB works,
- charging works.

Thus Apple accepts this replacement configuration for a normal restore.

## 13:14–16:11 — MacVDM can force DFU

Manual DFU still fails after restore.

Using **MacVDM**, the author is able to force the device/A3 into DFU.

In that forced DFU state, the host tools show **no normal A3 device information / ECID**.

The author hypothesizes that the absence of normal serialized identity is what allows this replacement chip to work across ROM/device combinations. That mechanism is not proven in the video; the observation is that the expected identity information is absent.

## 16:37–18:38 — Restore also succeeds from forced DFU

Even with the A3 forced into DFU by MacVDM, restore succeeds.

This is significant because ordinary personalized donor chips in mismatched configurations had previously triggered A3/TSS authorization failures.

## 19:24–23:08 — Practical conclusion

Observed advantages:
- works immediately after installation,
- no donor ROM transfer required,
- normal USB/touch/charging work,
- Apple restore succeeds.

Observed limitation:
- normal/manual DFU entry does not work.

Workaround:
- use **MacVDM** to force DFU when needed.

The author considers this the best tested solution when a same-model, same-revision donor A3 is unavailable.

His final comparison:

```
same model + same A3 revision donor
    → generally works

SN ↔ P mismatch
    → not acceptable / restore problems

“decrypted” replacement
    → works with existing ROM
    → Apple restore succeeds
    → manual PortDFU unavailable
    → MacVDM can force DFU
```

## Research significance

This video is especially relevant to serialization research because the successful replacement chip appears to avoid exposing the donor-specific PortDFU identity that caused problems in earlier experiments.

It does not prove how the chip was modified internally, only that the expected identity information was not visible in the forced-DFU test.
