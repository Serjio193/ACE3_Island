# Video 64 — Cross-model A3 swap: iPhone 15 A3 into iPhone 15 Plus

Cleaned technical transcript. Greetings, soldering dead time, music, OBS/Tahoe complaints, repetition, and non-technical narration removed.

## 1:25–7:05 — Setup and ROM observation

An A3 from an iPhone 15 is installed on an iPhone 15 Plus.

Before the swap, the author compares A3 external-ROM dumps from prior restore experiments and finds that the ROM contents are not identical even when the same A3 has been restored in another device. This reinforces that restore can rewrite/update the external A3 firmware image.

## 10:35–14:44 — Initial boot: PortDFU identifies as donor model

Because the recipient 15 Plus ROM does not match the iPhone 15 donor A3:
- the A3 does not boot normally,
- original-screen touch is absent,
- charging is absent,
- the host sees PortDFU.

3uTools identifies the PortDFU device as **iPhone 15**, even though the physical recipient is an iPhone 15 Plus.

The author attributes this to the donor A3’s programmed identity.

## 15:16–17:36 — Firmware/model conflict

Trying iPhone 15 Plus firmware first is rejected because the PortDFU-presented device appears to be an iPhone 15.

Trying iPhone 15 firmware then fails with restore error 3002 because the actual device is an iPhone 15 Plus.

## 18:56–24:00 — Failed restore temporarily boots the A3

After the failed restore attempt, USB/touch can temporarily return.

The author’s interpretation:
- the restore process uploaded a working A3 application image into RAM,
- it did **not** permanently program the external ROM.

Evidence: after removing battery power and rebooting, the A3 returns to PortDFU and again identifies as the donor iPhone 15.

So a failed restore can transiently bootstrap A3 enough to restore USB communication without making the fix persistent.

## 24:00–28:59 — Use temporary A3 boot to reach recovery

The author repeats the failed restore to temporarily boot the A3, keeps power applied, and enters recovery mode instead of restarting the controller.

Once the A3 is running and acting as the USB bridge, the computer can communicate with the AP directly.

In recovery mode the phone now identifies correctly as **iPhone 15 Plus**, allowing selection of the proper 15 Plus firmware.

## 29:31–33:39 — Checkpoint 131D / TSS refusal

The correct iPhone 15 Plus restore still fails.

Restore log:
- failure checkpoint: **131D**
- stage: **Update A3**
- TSS request occurs,
- server response includes: **“This device isn't eligible for the requested build”**
- and **“Declined to authorize this image on this device for this user.”**

Central result: even after reaching the recipient AP identity and selecting the proper 15 Plus firmware, Apple refuses the A3 update because the A3/device identity combination is not accepted.

## 34:29–34:43 — Matching donor ROM is not enough

The author states that copying the iPhone 15 ROM corresponding to the donor A3 can make the hardware operate normally, but Finder/iTunes restore still fails.

Therefore this is not merely an external-ROM-content problem.

## 35:28–39:30 — 3uTools succeeds where Finder fails

The device is flashed as an iPhone 15 Plus using 3uTools.

Observed result:
- flash succeeds,
- charging works,
- touch works,
- USB communication works,
- after full power removal/restart, A3 still boots, proving the external ROM was programmed successfully.

However, the resulting identity is still abnormal. The author observes an ECID that does not match the recipient phone and concludes that the device retains donor-A3 identity traces.

## 41:15–42:52 — Finder vs 3uTools behavior differs

The author compares restore logs around checkpoint 131D.

Observed:
- Finder/iTunes reaches A3/TSS authorization and is refused.
- 3uTools follows a different path and manages to obtain/use firmware that results in a working A3.

He does **not** establish exactly how 3uTools achieves this.

Important distinction:
- observed fact: 3uTools succeeds in this experiment,
- author hypothesis: 3uTools may be handling A3 authentication/TSS differently.

## 42:46–45:55 — Apple restore remains broken

A later Finder/iTunes restore attempt again fails at checkpoint 131D.

Final practical conclusion:
- A3 controllers should **not** be swapped across different iPhone models for customer repairs.
- Even if 3uTools makes the phone appear fully functional, standard Apple restore/update can remain broken.
- A future owner using Finder/iTunes could hit the same 131D failure.

For research, the important contrast is:

```
same-model/same-revision donor A3
    → Apple restore accepted

cross-model donor A3
    → Apple A3/TSS authorization rejected at 131D
    → 3uTools nevertheless succeeds
```
